\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Let $X$ be distributed uniformly on $\cube$ and let $Y$
be obtained by flipping each coordinate of $X$ with
probability $p$, independently.
In \cite{KumarC2013,CourtadeK2014}, Courtade and Kumar ask:
which bit $C$ computed from $Y$ is most informative about
$X$?
They conjecture that taking $C=Y_i$ for some $i\in[n]$
maximizes the information conveyed about $X$. In particular,
their Conjecture 1 states that 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ck}\tag{(CK)}
\muti{C}{X}\le\muti{Y_1}{X} = 1 - \entb{p}.
\end{equation}
They further remark that, despite its resemblance to a
simple homework exercise, the statement appears to
involve substantial depth. Indeed, even after extensive
efforts and partial progress
\cite{AnantharamBCJN2017,Arikan2009,BarnesO2020,ChenN2024,
FischerKRSS2004,JavanmardW2026,KindlerOW2015,LiM2021,
OrdentlichSW2015,ParnasRS2002,PichlerMP2016,Samorodnitsky2015,
Samorodnitsky2016,Sarbu2017,Sarbu2017b,YangW2018,Yu2024},
a full resolution of the conjecture remained elusive.

\input{figures/q-square}
In this work, we observe that if we study the inequality
parametrized by $\mu=\E[C]$, a delicate refinement
of \eqref{eq:ck} interpolating between Shannon and Tsallis
entropies emerges. This refinement admits a proof by induction
on the number of coordinates of $X$, $Y\!$.
Denoting the Tsallis entropy of $C$ by $\tsent{C} = 4\mu(1-\mu)$
and setting $\sI = \muti{Y_1}{X}$ and $\qI = (1-2p)^2$, we define
the progressive entropy of $C$ as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Q}\tag{(Q)}
  \Q=\qI\ent{C}-(\qI-\sI)\tsent{C}.
\end{equation}
With this definition in hand, we prove the following.
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:ck+}
Let $X$ be a uniform random bit string and $Y$ be obtained
by flipping each bit of $X$ with probability $p$,
independently. For a bit $C$ obtained from $Y\!$, we have
\begin{equation*}
\muti{C}{X}\le\qent{C}\le \muti{Y_1}{X}
\end{equation*}
for any $p\in[0,1]$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Comparison with earlier bounds}
\label{sec:earlier}
The strong data-processing inequality \cite{AhlswedeG1976}
gives $\muti{C}{X}\le\qI\ent{C}$,
the upper-right corner of \autoref{fig:q-square}.
Courtade and Kumar's \cite{CourtadeK2014} Conjecture~2
concerns the stronger upper bound $\sI\ent{C}$ at the upper-left
corner. This bound was later shown to be too strong in
general \cite{ChandarT2014,KindlerOW2015}.
Our refinement $\qent{C}$ subtracts a Tsallis correction
$(\qI-\sI)\,\tsent{C}$ from the strong data-processing bound
and sits between the two.

\subsection{Concurrent work}
\label{sec:concurrent}
Concurrently with our work, Ky and Tran~\cite{KyT2026},
Chen et al.~\cite{ChenGJL+2026}, and Mahdavifar and
Beirami~\cite{MahdavifarB2026} announced proofs of the
Courtade--Kumar conjecture. Their preprints were first
submitted to arXiv on September~21--22, 2026.
We had obtained a proof using Fourier analysis by
September~17, 2026, and completed writing down the simpler
induction proof, which we present here, by September~20, 2026.
Both versions are recorded in our public repository.\footnote{%
The Fourier proof:

The induction proof:
\url{https://github.com/deepinfra/courtade-kumar/commit/a07761c}.}

\subsection{The continuous case}
\label{sec:continuous}
We note that $\muti{C}{X}\le \Q$ is a surprisingly sharp
bound, which is already violated by a natural
generalization of the problem.
Let $X_\circ$ be distributed uniformly on the unit cube
$[0,1]^n$ and $Y_\circ$ be obtained by independently
resampling each coordinate of $X_\circ$ uniformly from
$[0,1]$ with probability $2p$.
Assume $C_\circ$ is a random bit obtained as a
function of $Y_\circ$ with $\mu=\E\sparen{C_\circ}$.

Let $T_\mu$ be the indicator of
$Y_{\circ,1}\le \mu$, so that $\E\sparen{T_\mu}=\mu$. In
\autoref{lem:ck+vscontinuous} we show that
$\qent{C_\circ} < \muti{T_\mu}{X_\circ}$ for $0<\mu<1$,
$\mu\ne1/2$, and $0<p<1/2$, which shows that $\qent{C_\circ}$
is too strong for the continuous problem. We conjecture that
the threshold functions maximize mutual information at
each fixed mean.
\begin{conjecture}
\label{conj:continuous}
Let $X_\circ$, $Y_\circ$ and $C_\circ$ be as constructed
and $T_\mu$ be the indicator of the event $Y_{\circ,1}\le\mu$
as above. It holds that
\begin{equation*}
  \muti{C_\circ}{X_\circ}\le\muti{T_\mu}{X_\circ}
\end{equation*}
for $0\le p\le1/2$ and $0\le\mu\le1$.
\end{conjecture}

Note that a proof of \autoref{conj:continuous} directly
implies \eqref{eq:ck}---though not our
\autoref{thm:ck+}---since given a strategy to output $C$
from $Y$ revealing $\muti{C}{X}$ bits of information, we can
quantize $Y_\circ$ by rounding each coordinate to $0$ or $1$
with threshold $1/2$ and apply the strategy to obtain
$C_\circ$. We get $\muti{C_\circ}{X_\circ}=\muti{C}{X}$
and it can be shown that
$\muti{T_\mu}{X_\circ}\le\muti{Y_1}{X}$.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}
All logarithms are base $2$, except $\ln$, which denotes the
natural logarithm. We write
\begin{align*}
 \entb{a}     &=a\log \frac{1}{a}+ (1-a)\log\frac{1}{1-a},\\
 \kldivb{a}{b}&=a\log\frac ab+(1-a)\log\frac{1-a}{1-b}
\end{align*}
for the binary entropy and divergence, respectively, with endpoint
values understood by continuity.
For $a\in[0,1]$ and $b\in[1/2,1)$, we repeatedly use
\begin{equation}
\tag{(U)}
\label{eq:U}
 \frac{2(a-b)^2}{\ln2}
 \le\kldivb{a}{b}
 \le\frac{\ln(1/b)}{(1-b)^2\ln2}(a-b)^2.
\end{equation}
The identity $\kldivb{a}{b}=\kldivb{1-a}{1-b}$ covers
$0<b<1/2$ as well. The lower bound also holds for $b=0,1$.

For the lower bound, the second derivative of the divergence in
$a$ is $1/(a(1-a)\ln2)\ge4/\ln2$, and its value and first
derivative vanish at $a=b$.
For the upper bound, Taylor's integral formula gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\kldivb{a}{b}}{(a-b)^2}
 &=\frac1{\ln2}\int_0^1
   \frac{1-s}{\nparen{b+s(a-b)}\nparen{1-b-s(a-b)}}
   \lebd s.
\end{align*}
The quotient extends continuously to $a=b$ and is convex in $a$,
since $1/(x(1-x))=1/x+1/(1-x)$ is convex on $(0,1)$.
It therefore attains its maximum at an endpoint.
The value at $a=1$ is at least the value at $a=0$ because
\begin{align*}
 -b^2\ln b+(1-b)^2\ln(1-b)&\ge0.
\end{align*}
Indeed, the expression vanishes at $b=1/2$ and $b=1$, and its
second derivative is $2\ln((1-b)/b)\le0$ on this interval.
The value at $a=1$ is the coefficient in the upper bound of
\eqref{eq:U}. Strict convexity of the quotient also makes this
bound strict when $0<a<1$ and $a\ne b$.

We will also use the Taylor expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binary-divergence-series}
 \kldivb{\frac{1+u}{2}}{\frac 12}
 =\frac1{2\ln2}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{u^{2k}}{k(2k-1)}
 \qquad(-1\le u\le1).
\end{equation}
Its value and first derivative vanish at $u=0$, and its second
derivative is $1/((1-u^2)\ln2)$.
Expanding this derivative as a geometric series and integrating
twice proves the identity for $|u|<1$; absolute convergence and
continuity include the endpoints.
